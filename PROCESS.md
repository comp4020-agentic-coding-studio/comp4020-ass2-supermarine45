# Process overview

## What I built
**SLOP4230 Retail Ethanol Economics** 
I designed a course that provides an analysis of alcohol consumption and purchasing behaviour. The course treats the bottle shop as a dataset and examines alcohol production and retail pricing through factors such as production costs, taxation, and retail margins. A series of practical assignments complements these topics, providing students with hands-on experience applying the concepts throughout the course.

## The Process

The spec required a subject that was sufficiently `narrow`, while nevertheless having enough depth to support a 12-week curriculum. I initially drew inspiration from topics featured in `Ig Nobel` Awards and explored several ideas, including household insects, capsicum taxonomy, and unmanned flying objects (UFOs). This is reflected in my first prototype, which focused on the topic of extinguishing household insects:

[`954f90e`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/954f90e).

I subsequently realized however that a comprehensive academic course requires sufficient grounding in real-world research. The initial topic therefore presented a limitation, as much of the content generated was made-up and lacked an adequate research basis. This also made iterative refinement difficult. Without established literature to ground the generated material, my feedback was largely limited to general instructions such as producing "better content", which didn't meaningfully improve the results. Thus, I abandoned the initial topic and pivoted to `Retail Alcohol Economics`:
[`dc14976`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/dc14976).

From this point, I specified the overall structure of the course, including its pages, assignments, grading scheme, and academic policies. I then used the agent to generate the weekly content and supporting visual material ([`9cff770`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/9cff770), [`8b27c21`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/8b27c21)).

A key requirement of the assignment was that the curriculum be coherent and avoid "AI slop". I found that the initial generated content did not meet this requirement. Consequently, I manually reviewed and revised the weekly material, while also identifying appropriate visualisations and other supporting elements ([`cba8ff6`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/cba8ff6), [`6afa721`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/6afa721), [`2851669`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/2851669), [`ba9a205`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/ba9a205)).

This iterative process highlighted a limitation of agentic coding framework: the interface and content generated by the agent were simplistic and could require manual refinements.

To further reduce "slop" content, I independently searched for relevant academic literature and theoretical concepts (e.g., WET policy) that could serve as foundations for individual weeks. These sources were then incorporated into the curriculum and used to replace or substantiate material generated by the agent: [`0f0eb37`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/0f0eb37)

The most challenging aspect of the assignment was thus, maintaining content academic coherence. Although the agent was useful for generating an initial structure, it was not reliable to perform this process autonomously.

More broadly, I observed that the agent, even when using Sonnet 5 with high reasoning effort, struggled with large and complex prompts, particularly those requiring the simultaneous generation of coherent course content, academic grounding, and visual design. At several stages, I therefore supplemented the agent's output with independent designs from other AI tools (i.e., Gemini Pro).

## Knowing It Was Right Before Accepting It
I considered the prototype ready only after manually proofreading the course content. This process revealed several issues that required manual correction, including:

* a grading scheme that was internally inconsistent;
* images with incorrect sizing or formatting ([`551822c`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-supermarine45/commit/551822c)); and
* a course schedule that did not adequately fit a 12-week teaching period

These checks were necessary to ensure that the generated prototype was internally consistent and apt as a complete university course.
