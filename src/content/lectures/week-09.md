**Core reading:** *Substrate-Borne Vibration as a Domestic Communication
Channel* (Bioacoustics, 2019)

A house is rarely silent, and Week 9 asks what the household's residents
are actually saying in the register humans mostly filter out. The week's
reading measures how cockroaches and other wall-void residents communicate
by vibration transmitted through timber framing and plasterboard rather
than through open air, meaning a faint tap or tick attributed to
"settling" is, in a measurable number of cases, a signal travelling
between two points in the same wall cavity. This reframes the household
soundscape as an occupied channel rather than empty background noise: the
house's structure is doing double duty as both shelter and shared
medium, carrying a conversation that predates any resident's arrival and
will outlast most residents' attempts to seal it off. Learning to hear a
tap as a signal, rather than dismissing it as decay, is this week's whole
skill.

## Outline

- vibration through timber and plasterboard as a real signal channel
- distinguishing structural settling from residents' own signalling
- the house's frame as shelter and shared medium at once
- treating background noise as evidence, not nuisance
