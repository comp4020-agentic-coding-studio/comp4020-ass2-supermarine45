**Core reading:** *Fermentation Kinetics of Ultra-Low-Cost Sugar Washes
in High-Volume Beverage Production* (Journal of Industrial Microbiology,
2020)

This week establishes the chemical and economic baseline of the course.
Ethanol is not viewed biologically; it is viewed purely as a fiscal
molecule. We examine the molecular efficiency of industrial yeast strains
(*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) in high-yield sugar washes to understand the
absolute minimum cost of production before federal taxation is applied.

*Key Concept:* Baseline fermentation margins and the absolute price floor
of industrial ethanol.

## Mandatory reading

- [Ethanol fermentation technologies from sugar and starch feedstocks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biotechadv.2007.09.002)
  — Bai, Anderson & Moo-Young, *Biotechnology Advances* 26(1), 2008,
  89–105. The industrial ferment this week's cost model assumes, and why
  *Saccharomyces* is still the organism used when *Zymomonas* returns the
  higher yield. Sections 2 and 3 are the ones you need.
- [Very high gravity (VHG) ethanolic brewing and fermentation: a research update](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10295-011-0999-3)
  — Puligundla, Smogrovicova, Obulam & Ko, *Journal of Industrial
  Microbiology & Biotechnology* 38(9), 2011, 1133–1144. Puts a number on
  the claim the lecture makes in words: energy is roughly 30% of
  production cost, and most of it sits downstream of the ferment rather
  than in it.
