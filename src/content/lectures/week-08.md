**Core reading:** *Visual Merchandising and the Intoxication Index:
Behavioral Biases* (Behavioral Economics Today, 2020)

An examination of behavioral pricing tiers and visual merchandising
tactics. We deconstruct how prominent "endcap" aisle displays utilize
psychological pricing (e.g., $19.99 vs. $20.00) and artificial scarcity
tags to obfuscate poor ABV-to-dollar ratios from the average consumer.

*Key Concept:* Cognitive biases in retail purchasing and visual
obfuscation.

## Mandatory reading

- [Sales impact of displaying alcoholic and non-alcoholic beverages in end-of-aisle locations: an observational study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2014.02.032)
  — Nakamura, Pechey, Suhrcke, Jebb & Marteau, *Social Science & Medicine*
  108, 2014, 68–73. A year of one store's product-level sales. End-of-aisle
  display raised volume by 23.2% for beer, 33.6% for wine and 46.1% for
  spirits — an effect the authors put at the equivalent of a 4–9% price
  cut. This is the paper to reach for when someone calls display
  decoration.
- [Alcohol and marketing](https://www.ias.org.uk/report/alcohol-and-marketing/)
  — Institute of Alcohol Studies. The wider marketing apparatus the
  endcap is one component of, which is the context your week 8 audit is
  a single observation within.
