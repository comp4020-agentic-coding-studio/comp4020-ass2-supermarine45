**Core reading:** *Externalities of Optimised Discount Alcohol Purchasing*
(Public Health Economics, 2021)

Ten weeks have built the tools for finding the mathematically optimal
alcohol-by-volume-to-price ratio, and week 11 is where the course
insists those tools are not, by themselves, a recommendation. The
week's reading models the public health externalities of purchasing
decisions optimised purely against the index — heavier concentration of
standard drinks per purchase, faster and cheaper access to intoxication
thresholds — and finds that these externalities scale with how strictly
the index is followed, not with how much money is spent. This is the
semester's one deliberate acknowledgment that the optimisation problem
this course teaches has a cost the index itself does not price in: a
model can be mathematically correct and still describe an outcome that
is a poor idea to actually execute, and the two judgements are not the
same calculation.

## Outline

- modelling externalities as a function of index adherence, not spend
- why standard-drinks-per-purchase is the variable that matters here
- an optimal index is a description, not automatically a recommendation
- holding a correct model and a bad outcome as two separate judgements
