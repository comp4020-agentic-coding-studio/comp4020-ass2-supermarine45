**Core reading:** *Cohabitation Conflict and De-escalation in Shared
Dwellings* (Journal of Applied Ethology, 2021)

Ten weeks of this course have described cohabitation working. Week 11 is
for when it does not: a bite, an allergic response, a resident whose
population has outgrown the accommodation the household can reasonably
offer. The week's reading proposes a de-escalation framework that orders
interventions by reversibility before it orders them by effectiveness —
sealing a specific entry point before treating a whole room, relocating a
nest before eliminating it, narrowing a boundary before widening one —
on the grounds that an irreversible response taken in the first panicked
hour tends to cost the household more cohabitation than the conflict
itself did. This is the closest the semester comes to acknowledging that
cohabitation has limits, and the reading is explicit that acknowledging a
limit honestly is not the same failure as abandoning the premise
entirely.

## Outline

- ordering interventions by reversibility before effectiveness
- sealing and relocating as first responses, not last ones
- what an irreversible response costs beyond the immediate conflict
- honestly acknowledging a limit without abandoning the premise
