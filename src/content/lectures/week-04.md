**Core reading:** *Volumetric Taxation and the Australian Cask Wine
Pricing Anomaly* (Australian Tax Forum, 2019)

Unlike spirits, which are taxed per liter of pure alcohol, Australian
wine is taxed at 29% of its wholesale value. This creates a highly
exploitable loophole for volume-agnostic taxation. We will model the
mathematical anomaly where an 80-standard-drink cask can legally retail
for less than a 20-standard-drink case of beer through aggressive
wholesale price minimization.

*Key Concept:* The Wine Equalisation Tax (WET) and volume-agnostic
federal loopholes.

## Mandatory reading

- [Excise and import taxes on wine versus beer and spirits: an international comparison](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1759-3441.2010.00064.x)
  — Anderson, *Economic Papers* 29(2), 2010, 215–228. The structural gap
  this week describes, measured across countries rather than asserted
  from one. Australia is not unusual in taxing wine differently; it is
  unusual in the size of the resulting difference.
- [The goon show: how the tax system works to subsidise cheap wine and alcohol consumption](https://australiainstitute.org.au/post/the-goon-show-how-the-tax-system-works-to-subsidise-cheap-wine-and-alcohol-consumption/)
  — The Australia Institute, 2015. The lecture's arithmetic on real
  Australian products: roughly four cents of WET per standard drink on
  the cheapest wine, against at least a dollar on beer and A$1.50 on
  spirits. Our worked figures are teaching numbers chosen to be easy to
  follow; these are the published ones, and you should be able to say why
  they differ.
