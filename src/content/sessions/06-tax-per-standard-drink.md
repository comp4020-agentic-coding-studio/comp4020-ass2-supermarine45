## Before the lab

Bring your week 4 WET calculations and add three products under federal
excise rather than WET: a spirit, a full-strength beer, and a
ready-to-drink can. Volume, ABV and shelf price for each.

## In the lab

**One axis.** The two regimes are not comparable in their own units — 29%
of wholesale value and dollars per litre of pure alcohol have nothing in
common. They become comparable the moment both are expressed per standard
drink, which is the entire reason week 2 fixed that unit before any tax
was discussed.

**Computing the premium.** Put all seven products on the axis. The spirits
figure will sit well above the wine figure, and the gap is what the
week's reading calls the spirits premium. Compute yours. It will not
match the reading's exactly, because your products are not its products;
a number you derived that differs from a published one, for reasons you
can name, is worth more here than a number you copied.

**What each design buys.** Volumetric excise closes the loophole week 4
found: two spirits of the same ABV pay the same tax regardless of price.
It pays for that with a flat burden markedly higher than wine's. Neither
property is an accident and neither is free, and the brief wants you to
say which trade-off you think the evidence supports.

**Drafting.** The last half hour is writing. The brief is due next week.

**Core reading:** *Volumetric Excise and the Distilled Spirits Premium*
(Australian Tax Policy Journal, 2020)

## Afterwards

You leave with seven products on one axis, your own spirits-premium
figure, and a drafted comparison paragraph — the brief's second required
element, from your own data rather than the reading's.
