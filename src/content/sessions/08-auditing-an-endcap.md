### Before the lab

From the outlet you mapped in week 7, record every product on one
promotional endcap: name, volume, ABV, price, and any promotional framing
the display carries — a was/now price, a multibuy, a "manager's special"
flash, a shelf-talker. Then record the equivalent products shelved
normally in the aisle immediately behind it.

### In the lab

**The arithmetic first.** Compute the index for both sets before
discussing either. The week's reading finds endcap placement and genuine
per-standard-drink value to be, if anything, negatively correlated —
negatively, not merely uncorrelated, which is a stronger claim and worth
testing against your own two sets rather than accepting.

**Then the framing.** Go back to what you recorded about the display
itself. A was/now price anchors against a figure you cannot verify. A
multibuy shifts the unit of comparison from price-per-drink to
price-per-transaction. A category adjacency puts a poor index next to a
good one so the pair averages. Name which devices were on your endcap.

**The uncomfortable half.** Before you computed anything, which display
did you expect to be better value? Most people expect the endcap, and the
reading's argument is that this expectation is the product being sold.
Being wrong here is the normal result and is worth recording honestly in
your working — it is evidence about the display, not about you.

### Afterwards

You leave with a paired dataset — endcap against aisle — and a written
note of which framing device moved your judgement. Week 9 stops doing this
by hand.
