### Before the lab

Bring your week 10 solver output — the constrained selection it produced
for a fifty-person event — and read this week's lecture beforehand. The
lab is mostly writing, and it goes badly without the reading.

### In the lab

**What is being asked.** The portfolio requires a stated position on the
externalities raised in week 11, applied to your event's scale. Not a
disclaimer, not a paragraph of throat-clearing before the real answer —
a position, with the same evidentiary standard as your tax argument.

**The first failure: the hedge.** A statement that says the optimisation
should be applied "responsibly" has said nothing, because it names no
variable and forbids no outcome. Test yours: does it identify something
specific your model does, and does it say what follows? If it would read
identically under any inventory whatsoever, it is a hedge.

**The second failure: the joke.** A statement that treats the externality
as the funny part of an otherwise serious document has also said nothing,
and has additionally told the reader not to trust the rest. Test yours: is
there a sentence you wrote because it was amusing rather than because it
was true?

**What the reading actually claims.** Externalities scale with adherence
to the index, not with spend — so the honest version of the argument is
about how strictly your portfolio is followed, and the multiplication by
guest count is where an individual heuristic becomes an event-scale
quantity. That multiplication is the specific claim your statement can
make that a shopper-scale one cannot.

### Afterwards

You leave with a drafted statement that has survived both tests, and one
named consequence at event scale. Week 12 puts it in front of the room.
