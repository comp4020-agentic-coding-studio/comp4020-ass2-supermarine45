## Before the lab

Bring current shelf prices for two products from your catalogue: a
four-litre bag-in-box cask and a 750 mL bottle of the same varietal, or as
close to the same varietal as your retailer stocks. Bring the volume and
ABV for both. You do not need to have decomposed anything yet — that is
what the lab is for.

## In the lab

**The four terms.** Every price this course examines splits into
production cost, packaging and freight, category tax, and margin. Week 1
established the first. This lab does the second and estimates the fourth
by difference, which is the honest way to do it: margin is the term nobody
publishes, so it is the term you are left holding once the others are
accounted for.

**Working the cask.** The week's reading gives per-litre packaging and
freight figures for bag-in-box against glass. Apply them to your two
products and the gap appears immediately — glass is heavy, it is fragile,
and it is shipped as mostly air. Then apply the Wine Equalisation Tax at
29% of wholesale value to both, which week 4 covers properly and this lab
takes on trust for now.

**What is left over.** Subtract. The residual is margin plus whatever your
estimates got wrong, and the point of the exercise is that you cannot yet
tell those two apart. Write down the number and write down your doubt
about it; both go in your working.

**Core reading:** *Margin Structures in Bag-in-Box Wine Distribution*
(Retail Economics Review, 2018)

## Afterwards

You leave with two decomposed prices and a residual you can defend as an
estimate rather than a fact. Week 4 replaces the tax term's placeholder
with the real mechanism, and the residual shrinks.
