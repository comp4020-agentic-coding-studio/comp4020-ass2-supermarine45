## Before the session

Bring the standard-drink conversion and index value you computed in session
1, and the one decision behind it you are least confident about — a
rounding choice, a promotional price you weren't sure whether to use, a
product you guessed the ABV for.

## In the session

Short demos of each student's session-1 numbers, then feedback aimed at the
decision rather than the arithmetic. Idris works through one live example
to demonstrate what "showing your working" should look like in practice:
cask wine against the Wine Equalisation Tax.

**The worked example.** A four-litre cask returns a lower
price-per-standard-drink than almost anything else on the shelf, for two
compounding reasons. Bag-in-box packaging removes most of bottled wine's
glass and freight cost per litre, and the Wine Equalisation Tax is levied
at 29% of wholesale value rather than per litre of alcohol — so a $2
wholesale cask returns only a few cents of tax per standard drink, while a
bottle at ten times the wholesale price returns proportionally the same
rate but a far higher absolute tax per standard drink sold. The example
produces a genuine price-floor anomaly: an 80-standard-drink cask can
legally retail for less than a 20-standard-drink case of beer. Weeks 3 and
4 cover both halves of this in full; the session only uses it to show what
a defensible worked answer looks like before your own numbers are checked
against the same standard.

**Required reading:** *Volumetric Taxation and the Australian Cask Wine
Pricing Anomaly* (Australian Tax Review, 2019)

## Afterwards

If attendance or participation is worth marks, that rule is stated on the
[assessment page](/assessments/) rather than repeated here. What leaves the
session is feedback on the one decision you brought, and a second worked
example — cask wine and the WET — to check your own method against once
weeks 3 and 4 cover the same ground in full.
