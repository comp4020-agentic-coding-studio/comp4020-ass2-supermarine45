Idris is a third-year PhD candidate at Slop University, supervised by
Marisol Quaye. His doctoral research sits at the intersection of
microeconomics and macroeconomics, studying how Australian consumers
respond to price signals, taxation structure, and point-of-sale
merchandising in retail alcohol markets — work that draws directly on the
fieldwork behind week 8's argument that endcap merchandising and genuine
value are, if anything, negatively correlated.

His methodological toolkit spans applied econometrics — difference-in-
differences and regression-discontinuity designs fitted to retail scanner
data — survey design and fielding, and physiological data collection: his
doctoral fieldwork tracked consumer gaze and stress response inside
suburban liquor outlets, mapping foot-traffic density against the
ABV-to-dollar ratio of the display each shopper paused in front of. Before
starting the PhD he spent six years as a logistics consultant, optimising
bag-in-box wine distribution networks for minimum cardboard deformation
under pallet load — an unglamorous problem that turned out to explain a
surprising share of cask wine's retail margin, and the reason week 3's
case study still comes up in his supervision meetings.

Recent working papers include *Ocular Tracking and Endcap Concealment in
Suburban Liquor Retail* (Journal of Retail Behavioural Science, 2022) and
*Heuristic Substitution Under Promotional Framing: Evidence from
Australian Liquor Retailers* (Australasian Journal of Consumer Research,
2024).

Idris teaches the course's even weeks, from week 2's standard-drink metric
through to week 12's capstone, and runs the practical review labs in
between — including the first review, which checks each student's early
standard-drink and index calculations before the semester's assessments
come to depend on them.
