Marisol is an Associate Professor at Slop University, specialising in
economics and governance. Her research programme sits at the boundary of
regulatory design and consumer decision-making — how a tax or labelling
regime reshapes not just a shelf price but the choice a consumer makes in
response to it — a question week 1's production-cost baseline and week 3's
margin analysis are both built to establish before governance enters the
picture in later weeks.

Her broader portfolio spans behavioural economics and neuroeconomics,
examining the cognitive and physiological mechanisms behind everyday
purchasing decisions. Recent publications include *Regulatory Design and
the Behavioural Response to Ad Valorem Taxation* (Journal of Economic
Governance, 2019), *Heuristics and Bias in Discount-Driven Purchase
Decisions* (Journal of Behavioural Economics, 2022), and *Neural
Correlates of Price-Ratio Judgement Under Time Pressure* (Neuroeconomics
Review, 2024).

She currently leads the Algorithmic Governance Lab, where a standing set
of monitoring scripts tracks real-time pricing and promotional anomalies
across the ready-to-drink retail sector — infrastructure that feeds
directly into the questions week 10's index formula and the semester's
data scraping exercise are designed to make tractable by hand before
students see the automated version.

Marisol designed the course's twelve-week arc and teaches its odd weeks,
from week 1's fermentation kinetics through to week 11's externality
framework — the production and governance half of the semester that Idris
Fenn, her third-year PhD candidate and the course's tutor, applies to
merchandising and algorithmic behaviour in the weeks between.
