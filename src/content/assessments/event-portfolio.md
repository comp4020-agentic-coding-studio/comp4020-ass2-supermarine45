## The brief

> Design the mathematically optimal, cheapest possible alcohol inventory
> for a 50-person student event, and pitch it in ten slides with a budget
> spreadsheet behind it.

This is the semester's synthesis assessment, and it is built to punish
shortcuts the earlier weeks specifically warned against. A greedy,
single-SKU-optimal inventory clears the budget constraint trivially and
fails the brief, exactly as week 10 predicts once a minimum-variety
requirement is added; a floor plan that ignores week 7's placement
findings will misjudge how easily the inventory can actually be
purchased; and a pitch that treats the optimisation as ethically neutral
has not engaged with week 11's argument that externalities scale with
how strictly the index is followed. A strong portfolio states its budget
and variety constraints up front, shows the constrained-optimisation
working behind the final SKU list rather than just the list, and
addresses the event-scale externality question directly rather than
leaving it for the audience to raise.

## What you submit

A ten-slide pitch deck and an accompanying budget spreadsheet, submitted
together, itemising every SKU, quantity, and price against the stated
budget and variety constraints.

This assessment uses the alternative marking mode: a holistic judgement
against the brief rather than weighted criteria. A defensible, honestly
argued portfolio that survives the constrained-optimisation and
externality questions outweighs a cheaper inventory that only survives
the budget line.
