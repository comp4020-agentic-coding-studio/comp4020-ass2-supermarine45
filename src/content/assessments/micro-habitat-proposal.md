## The brief

> Design a modification to the dwelling that measurably improves quality of
> life for one identified resident species, without disrupting how a human
> resident already uses that space.

Week 5 established that ordinary construction already produces
infrastructure — wall voids, skirting clearances — that residents use
without it having been designed for them. This proposal asks you to extend
that infrastructure deliberately: a wider gap at a specific skirting
junction, a stable humidity pocket added to an underused void, a boundary
channel sited using week 10's chemical-signalling method rather than guessed
at. The proposal is not marked on ambition. A modification that would
require rewiring, replumbing, or a week without access to the kitchen fails
the feasibility criterion regardless of how much it might help the resident
in question, and a modification that improves the resident's territory at
the cost of a human resident's existing use of that same space fails
non-disruptiveness on the same grounds.

## What you submit

A written proposal: the identified resident species, the specific
modification, the materials and steps required to build it, and a stated
measure of improvement — more stable humidity, a shorter foraging distance,
reduced human-resident contact — that could plausibly be checked after the
fact.

The `marking:` block above weights demonstrated improvement highest, since a
proposal that is buildable and unobtrusive but does not actually improve
anything for the resident it names has satisfied the constraints without
answering the brief.
