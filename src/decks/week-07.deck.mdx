## The sink after the household sleeps

Once the household stops using the kitchen, the sink's basin does not
become irrelevant. It becomes, for several hours, the single most
predictable water source in the dwelling:

1. daytime — sink is disturbed constantly, unusable as a resource
2. evening — disturbance drops, residual water remains if unwiped
3. overnight — a wiped sink offers nothing; a damp one offers hours of it

---

## What the reading measured

- hydration visits logged against sink state across many households
- visits cluster tightly on nights with standing water present
- visits drop sharply, not gradually, once the sink is wiped dry
- the drop is immediate — no adjustment period, no lingering visits

# The sink's overnight state is a social contract, not a mess

---

## Reading the visit-state relationship

| Sink state overnight | Recorded hydration visits | Household effort required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Standing water left | Highest | None |
| Damp but drained | Moderate | Minimal |
| Fully wiped dry | Lowest | One wipe |

A single, low-effort choice governs the entire pattern. Nothing about it
requires ongoing vigilance.

---

## Why "sociology" is the right word here

- the relationship is legible: one variable, one predictable response
- it is negotiated nightly, not fought over once and settled
- withdrawing the resource is not eviction, it is renegotiation
- this is the clearest case all semester for treating a household choice
  as a term in an ongoing relationship, not a one-off intervention

---

## Common misreadings

- treating a damp sink as evidence of failure rather than as a variable
- assuming visits will taper off gradually if water is left "just once more"
- confusing the sink's social role with a hygiene question — the reading
  measures neither disease risk nor cleanliness, only visit pattern
- forgetting that the same variable governed week 3's corner webs and
  week 6's crumb trails: control the resource, and the pattern follows
