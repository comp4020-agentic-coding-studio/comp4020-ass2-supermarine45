## Learning objectives

## What the objective function says

Week 10 maximises **total standard drinks subject to a budget**.

Read that sentence literally. It contains:

- a quantity to maximise
- a constraint

It does not contain a person, a timeframe, a consumption rate, or a
consequence. Those are not oversights in the formulation — they are
simply not in it.

# Adherence, not spend

---

## Why that variable, specifically

- spending more does not concentrate standard drinks per purchase
- **following the index** does, by construction — that is what it
  optimises for
- so the externality tracks how strictly the rule is applied
- which means the model's own success metric is the thing that scales it

This is an uncomfortable result and the reading does not soften it.

---

## Two judgements, not one

A model can be:

- **mathematically correct** — the arithmetic checks, the constraint
  binds, the optimum is the optimum
- **describing an outcome that is a poor idea to execute**

These are evaluated by different methods and neither settles the other.
Ten weeks of this course established the first. None of it establishes
the second.

---

## The two ways this argument fails

- **the hedge** — "apply the optimisation responsibly". Names no
  variable, forbids no outcome, would read identically under any
  inventory whatsoever.
- **the joke** — treating the externality as the amusing part of a
  serious document, which additionally tells the reader not to trust the
  rest of it.

Your portfolio statement has to survive both tests. The week 11 lab
applies them.

---

## What a position looks like

It names something specific your model does, and says what follows.

At event scale the multiplication is explicit: the externality is
multiplied by the guest count, which is a quantity you chose and can
defend or revise. That is a claim a shopper-scale statement cannot make.

---

## Common errors

- writing a disclaimer and calling it a position
- concluding the optimisation should not be taught, which is not what the
  reading argues
- treating "the index is correct" as settling whether to follow it
- deferring the whole question to the reader
