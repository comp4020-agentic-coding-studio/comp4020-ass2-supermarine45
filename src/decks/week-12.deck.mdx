# Variety stops being slack

---

## Why the constraint moves

For one shopper:

- the best-index product, bought repeatedly, is usually an acceptable basket
- so variety is **slack**, and greedy often coincides with optimal

For fifty people:

- that same single-SKU inventory is trivial to construct and reliably wrong
- so variety **binds**, and greedy stops coinciding

---

## The trap in one line

The cheapest defensible answer and the cheapest answer are different
documents.

A portfolio that maximises standard drinks per dollar and stops there has
solved week 10's problem, not week 12's — and the room will find the gap
in the first question.

---

## What does not scale away

Week 11's externality does not disappear at event scale. It is
**multiplied by the guest count**.

That multiplication is why the reading treats event purchasing as a
harder problem rather than a bigger one, and why your externality
statement has a specific quantity available to it that a shopper-scale
statement does not.
