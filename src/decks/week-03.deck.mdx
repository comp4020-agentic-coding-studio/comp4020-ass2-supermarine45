## Learning objectives

By the end of this lecture you should be able to:

- trace bag-in-box distribution from bulk producer to retail shelf
- estimate packaging and freight cost per litre for two formats
- explain why cask returns the lowest ratio of any wine format
- hold a product's reputation and its measured efficiency apart

---

## Two paths to the shelf

**Bottled:** bulk producer → bottler → distributor → wholesaler →
retailer

**Bag-in-box:** bulk producer → filler → retailer

Fewer intermediaries, each taking a margin. The reading's central finding
is that this structural difference, not the wine, is the story.

---

## What the packaging costs

| Cost component | Glass, 750 mL | Bag-in-box, 4 L |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| Container per litre | high | low |
| Mass shipped per litre of wine | high | low |
| Breakage and handling loss | material | negligible |
| Shelf space per standard drink | high | low |

Glass is heavy, fragile, and shipped as mostly air. Every one of those is
a cost that arrives at the shelf.

# Passed through, not pocketed

---

## The finding that matters

The reading's surprise is not that cask is cheaper. It is **where the
saving goes**.

- lower packaging cost per litre is largely **passed through** to price
- it is not absorbed as additional retailer margin
- so the format's cost advantage shows up in the standard-drink price
- which makes cask the lowest ratio of any wine format sold

---

## Reputation against measurement

- cask wine's shelf reputation is poor and reasonably well known
- its measured price-per-standard-drink is the best in the category
- **both statements are true at once**
- this course only makes the second kind of claim, and week 8 is about
  how displays are built to blur the difference

---

## Common errors

- attributing the price gap to wine quality, which the reading explicitly
  tests for and does not find
- comparing a 4 L cask against a 750 mL bottle by price rather than by
  standard drinks
- assuming a lower input cost implies a higher retail margin
- treating "lowest ratio in the category" as a purchasing recommendation
  rather than a measurement
