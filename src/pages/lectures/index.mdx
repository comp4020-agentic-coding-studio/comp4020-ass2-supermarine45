Twelve weeks, one argument: a retail price is production cost, plus
category-specific tax, plus margin, plus whatever the shelf position is
worth — and each of those four terms can be measured separately.

Weeks 1 to 3 establish the baseline the other three sit on top of. Weeks 4
to 6 add taxation and set the ad valorem Wine Equalisation Tax against
volumetric excise. Weeks 7 and 8 add the floor plan and the display on it.
Weeks 9 and 10 turn the whole thing into a dataset and then into an
optimisation. Week 11 asks what that optimisation costs, and week 12 scales
it to an event.

Each week is one page. The lecture makes the argument; the lab immediately
below it computes the same thing by hand on a product you chose, because a
term you have not calculated yourself is a term you cannot defend in an
assessment. The slides for the week are linked from the top of its page and
from its card below.

Every week names two kinds of reading. The **core reading** is the week's set
text, and is where its argument comes from. The **mandatory reading** is
published work you can go and read: each entry is a live link to the paper,
report or guideline, and what the week asks you to take from it.
