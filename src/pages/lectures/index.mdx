Over twelve weeks, we advance a singular, unbroken argument: a retail price is a composite of four calculable vectors: baseline production cost, category-specific federal taxation, retail margin, and spatial shelf-position value. Every module is dedicated to measuring and exploiting these specific terms.

**The Twelve-Week Progression**

* **Phase I: The Chemical Baseline (Weeks 1–3):** We establish the absolute price floor of industrial fermentation and quantify the logistical margin efficiencies of alchol distribution.
* **Phase II: Regulatory Exploitation (Weeks 4–6):** We map federal taxation mechanics, pitting the Wine Equalisation Tax (WET) loophole directly against the punitive volumetric excises applied to distilled spirits.
* **Phase III: Spatial & Algorithmic Optimization (Weeks 7–10):** We move to the retail floor, mapping the spatial obfuscation of suburban liquor outlets before deploying automated scrapers to mathematically maximize the ABV-to-dollar index across live datasets.
* **Phase IV: Synthesis & Ethics (Weeks 11–12):** We confront the public health externalities of hyper-efficient alcohol procurement before scaling your optimization algorithms to macro-event logistics.

**Pedagogy: Lectures, Labs, and Decks**
Theory without practice is mere speculation. Each weekly module consists of a core lecture, a linked presentation deck, and an Applied Computational Lab. In these labs, you will compute the week's specific vector by hand using a real-world SKU you have selected. An economic term you have not calculated yourself is a term you cannot defend in your final assessment.

**Course Literature**
Every week draws upon two distinct tiers of literature. The **Core Theory** gave the baseline for the week's lecture. The **Mandatory Literature** consists of linked, published papers, tax guidelines, and health-economics reports. You are expected to read and extract the empirical data from these external sources to ground your analysis in reality.
